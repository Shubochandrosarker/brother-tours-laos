The Region

Where Laos becomes slow.

North to south across Laos, by river and road, at the pace the country keeps. A journey carried by a licensed Lao host, with named hotels, named meals, and time built in to do nothing well.

Designed for the slow traveler who wants to feel Laos rather than photograph it.

Region at a glance

Best seasonNov – Feb (cool dry)

Best places to visit

Places that stay with you.

Luang Prabang

UNESCO heritage city — morning alms, the old royal palace.

Nong Khiaw

Karst-valley village — viewpoints and kayak country.

Vang Vieng

Limestone lagoons, quieter than its reputation.

Plain of Jars

Iron-age stone vessels across the Xieng Khouang plateau.

Pakse & the Bolaven

Coffee plateau, waterfalls, and the road south.

Vientiane

The riverside capital — temples and a slow evening market.

4,000 Islands

Where the Mekong braids — Don Det, Don Khone, the falls.

The Nam Ou

A slow boat threading the river between karst walls.

Field notes

Travel tips from our guides.

01 Pack a light layer.November mornings can drop to 12 °C in the highlands; days warm to 24 °C.
02 Book the slow boat ahead.Private river boats fill weeks ahead in the cool-dry months.
03 Respect the alms ceremony.Stand quiet and at a distance. We brief you privately the morning of.
04 Carry small kip notes.Village markets rarely break large notes.
Good to know

Travelers frequently ask.

Is this region safe for travelers?
Yes. Laos is a calm, welcoming country. Your host is with you throughout.
How many days do I need here?
Seven to fourteen days lets the region unfold at its own pace.
What is the best month to visit?
November to February is cool and dry; the green season brings fewer travelers and lower light.
Can I combine it with another region?
Yes — we design north-to-south journeys and cross-border routes on request.
